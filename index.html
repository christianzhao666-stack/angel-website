02 — Process

Ten steps, from arrival to export.

From incoming inspection to container loading, we check quality along the way, and pianos are shipped only after passing a final inspection.

  1. Incoming inspection

    Pianos arrive at our central warehouse, where each one is assessed; only pianos we judge suitable for reconditioning enter the workshop.

  2. Deep cleaning

    Interior and exterior surfaces are thoroughly cleaned to remove years of dust, stains, and residue.

  3. Case refinishing & polishing

    Cases are carefully sanded and polished to reduce scratches and blemishes and bring back a high-gloss finish.

  4. Keytop polishing & replacement

    Keytops are polished for a smooth feel; cracked or damaged keytops are replaced with closely matched materials.

  5. Action component replacement

    Technicians replace worn action parts as needed and adjust each action by hand.

  6. Complete piano rebuild

    For clients requiring a full upgrade, strings, hammers, and other core components are rebuilt and carefully adjusted.

  7. Action regulation & keybed setup

    Key weight, height, and dip are measured and regulated with reference to the manufacturer’s specifications.

  8. Regulation, voicing & tuning

    Note by note, working towards even tone colour and balance across the range, followed by careful tuning.

  1. Pre-shipment final inspection

    Each piano is checked before shipment. Issues we find are reworked, and only pianos that pass are approved for shipping.

  2. Container loading & export

    Refurbished pianos are professionally packed into ocean containers and shipped from the Port of Dalian to partners worldwide. We can quote FOB Dalian or CIF to your destination port.

04 — Since 1861 · A Treaty Port

Our City — Yingkou

Angel Musical is based in Yingkou, Liaoning, where the Liao River meets Liaodong Bay. Under the Treaty of Tianjin (1858), the river port of Newchwang was designated for foreign trade; because the river there had silted, the port opened downstream at Yingkou in 1861 — the first treaty port in Northeast China. Western traders kept the treaty's name, Newchwang.

In 1864 the Newchwang Customs House opened here, the first customs office in the region. For decades the city was a principal outlet for Manchuria's trade; consulates, trading houses, banks and churches followed, and buildings from that era still stand along the old riverfront.

Today the Port of Yingkou, with its deep-water Bayuquan harbour, is one of the largest in Northeast China, and Dalian lies about 200 km to the south. We work from a city that has traded with the world for more than 160 years.
